On Qatar Airways economy, the checked bag weight depends on your fare type and where you're flying.

**Routes other than Africa or the Americas (weight-based):**
- Economy Lite: 20 kg (44 lb)
- Economy Classic: 25 kg (55 lb)
- Economy Convenience: 30 kg (66 lb)
- Economy Comfort: 35 kg (77 lb)

[These are the checked baggage weight limits for economy on routes outside Africa and the Americas.](https://www.qatarairways.com/en-iq/baggage/allowance.html)

**Flights to or from Africa or the Americas (piece-based):**
- Economy Lite: 1 piece up to 23 kg (50 lb)
- Economy Classic and above: 2 pieces up to 23 kg (50 lb) each

[Each individual bag can't exceed 32 kg (70 lb) on any route.](https://www.qatarairways.com/en-iq/baggage/allowance.html)

Your fare type is shown on your booking, and you can check your exact allowance in the Qatar Airways manage booking page.

Short answer: yes, an American citizen needs a visa to work in the UK in healthcare for more than 6 months. But the page I found doesn't say that about US citizens by name, so that part comes from GOV.UK's general rules rather than a US-specific line.

The relevant GOV.UK page is the [Health and Care Worker visa overview](https://www.gov.uk/health-care-worker-visa). It says:

- [A Health and Care Worker visa allows medical professionals to come to or stay in the UK to do an eligible job with the NHS, an NHS supplier or in adult social care.](https://www.gov.uk/health-care-worker-visa)
- [You must have a confirmed job offer before you apply for your visa.](https://www.gov.uk/health-care-worker-visa) The job also has to be with a UK employer approved by the Home Office.
- You can [take on additional work in certain circumstances](https://www.gov.uk/health-care-worker-visa), and you can apply to settle permanently in the UK</parameter>... 

Let me correct that last bit: the page says you can apply to settle permanently after 5 years, and that you cannot change jobs or employer without updating the visa.

GOV.UK's general visa check ([Check if you need a UK visa](https://www.gov.uk/check-uk-visa)) is the tool that asks your nationality and how long you plan to work. It has a "six months or less" branch for work, which is why a stay longer than 6 months points to a work visa. I didn't run that tool for a US passport, so I can't quote its US result.
